# Race Plan Viewer: methodology

The page lays out five plans for 5km to 10km racing from a race result, with paces and load: Daniels' 5K to 10K plan for 40 to 50 miles a week, his plan for 60 to 70 miles a week, Davis's Full-Spectrum 10k schedule, and Pfitzinger's 8K-10K Schedules 1, 2 and 3 (30 to 42, 45 to 57 and 60 to 76 miles a week). The race, time, units and starting CTL are shared. Load (TSS, CTL, ATL, ramp) is calculated as on the Marathon Plan Viewer: TSS = hours x (threshold pace / pace)^2 x 100 with threshold as race(16), CTL with a 42-day and ATL with a 7-day time constant, and a blank starting CTL taken as week 1's TSS divided by 7.

The race on the last day is scored at its predicted pace and added to the final week's TSS, time and distance. CTL and ATL for that week are for the morning of the race, and the page gives the values after the race as well.

### Paces

The paces are those of the Marathon Plan Viewer's Daniels plans: easy 74% of 5km speed as written (or your own easy-pace setting), M the Riegel marathon prediction, T = race(17), I = race(4), R = race(1.7). The slower-runner option caps T reps at 6, 15 and 20 minutes for 1, 2 and 3 miles, as on that page.

H (hard) is effort-based. The author read the book's description as a pace for a 10 to 12 minute effort, meant for cases such as training at altitude; the screenshots do not define it and the book gives no pace formula. The page uses I pace for H, as the author decided on 2026-11-12. The H pace box offers halfway between T and I instead.

### Notation

A number before E, M or T is miles. Reps are metres, miles or minutes at the pace of the letter. Jogs run at easy pace and count towards distance and TSS. Rests are standing and score 0. "1K" is 1000m.

### Assumptions where the pages are ambiguous

- 4 to 6 x 1,200m I (Table 11.2, Phase III week 4) is set to 5 reps, and 5 to 8 x 1K I (Phase III week 2) to 6, both within the book's 24-minute limit.
- Where a Phase IV race-week session ends without an easy segment (the printed no-race version has "+ 2 E" or "+ 1 E"), the same easy mileage is used for the race-week version.
- Phase IV week 2, Table 11.2, no-race Tuesday: read as steady 3T + 2E + 2T + 2E.
- Races carry no extra warm-up or cool-down.
- 3 x 2T + 2 min rests (Table 11.2, Phase III week 4, Saturday) is read as 3 reps of 2 miles with 2 minutes' rest.

## Source logs for the half-marathon plan

The reconciled plan below was built from four athlete training logs in Davis's sub-70 half-marathon article (RunningWritings, relative-paces PDFs): 1:08:20 on 70 to 80 mi a week (12 weeks), 1:09:00 on 80 to 90 mi (20 weeks), 1:08:40 on 65 to 70 mi (8 weeks) and 1:07:20 on 60 to 70 mi (11 weeks). The logs are not shown on the page. The article text has no day-by-day calendars.

Effort words in the logs (very easy, easy, moderate, strong) were fitted by me to the logs' stated weekly km: 56, 72, 80 and 90% of half-marathon speed, with easy-to-moderate at 76% and moderate-to-strong at 85%. Total distance came within about 2% of the stated total over all 51 weeks; single weeks differed by up to about 15%. This is my calibration, not a figure the article gives. The plan's own notes use the same percentages. One log mixed percentages of a lower current pace with its eventual pace; where only the current figure was given, I scaled it to the eventual pace (x0.947 to x0.967) before using it as a reference.

### Assumptions where the pages are ambiguous

- Jog recoveries: LT uses the printed minutes. 8K-10K race-pace intervals use 50% of interval time (printed). 3K-5K intervals use 70%, the middle of the printed 50 to 90%.

- The 4 minutes between sets of 200s is standing rest (scores 0, adds to time). The printed "jog 200 m recovery" between reps is run at recovery pace.
- Intervals with a list of lengths are run in the order shown, with a jog after every interval except the last.
- Post-race recovery week is included as week 13.

### Target heart rate intensities, Table 1.2 (p. 11)

Transcribed from a photograph. Not used by the page yet.

| Run type | Maximal heart rate (%) | Heart rate reserve (%) |
|---|---|---|
| VO2max | 94-98 | 92-97 |
| Lactate threshold | 80-91 | 75-88 |
| Endurance | 74-84 | 65-78 |
| General aerobic | 70-81 | 62-75 |
| Recovery | < 76 | < 70 |

Target heart rate = resting heart rate + prescribed percent of heart rate reserve (HRR = maximal minus resting). The book's example: maximal 190, resting 50, reserve 140. Endurance run: 74-84% of maximum is 141-160 bpm; 65-78% of reserve is 141-159 bpm. The book says the lactate threshold range is broad: less experienced runners sit at the lower end, more experienced at the higher end. Heat: in warm conditions start at the low end of the range as heart rate drifts up; on a low-humidity day in the 70s F (about 21-26 C) raise the zones by 2 to 5 bpm; on a high-humidity day in the 70s or a low-humidity day in the 80s F (27-32 C) raise them by 5 to 10 bpm; on a high-humidity day in the 80s or 90s F (27-37 C) VO2max and lactate threshold work will not be hard enough to be useful and carries overheating risk, so move it to another day.

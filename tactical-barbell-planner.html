    How this is calculated

    Enter a tested 1RM, or a set to estimate one. The estimate is the average of the Brzycki and Epley formulas applied to reps plus reps in reserve, and is most reliable for sets of about 3–5 reps taken to failure. The training max is a percentage of that 1RM, and the Operator percentages apply to it. Leave it at 100% to use the 1RM directly.

    Standard mode applies the programme percentage to the max. High-rep mode keeps each week's reps in reserve and changes the reps to 10, 8 or 6, which lowers the percentage. Reps in reserve are estimated from the same two formulas, so treat them as a guide. They get less reliable above about 10 reps.

    How the other programmes are calculated

    The tables show the sessions you would do if every one went as written. Weights start from the numbers you enter and rise by each routine's own per-session step. Stalls, deloads and stage changes depend on your reps, so a table is a projection, not a prescription. Weights marked * move by a rule, not by session.

    The sources give pounds. Steps are converted to practical kilograms: 2.5 lb becomes 1.25 kg, 5 lb becomes 2.5 kg and 10 lb becomes 5 kg. The 5/3/1 training max is 90% of a 1RM estimated from your set, using the same Brzycki and Epley average as above. Rules are summarised in our own words. Check each source for the full text.
